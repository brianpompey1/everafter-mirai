Questions couples ask us first

Do we need a Florida marriage license?

Yes — for a legal marriage in Florida, you obtain the license yourselves from any Florida county clerk before your ceremony, and bring the valid, effective, unexpired license with you on the day. We complete the officiant’s section and follow the clerk’s return procedure.

We offer marriage-license guidance, not legal advice. The issuing clerk is always the authority on current requirements and fees.

Can we be married anywhere in Florida?

A Florida marriage license is generally valid throughout the state. The practical limit is the location itself: parks, beaches, gardens, and private venues can require reservations, permits, admission fees, or approval of outside vendors.

Tell us where you’re imagining and we’ll tell you what it takes to marry there.

Do we need witnesses?

Follow the guidance your issuing county clerk gives you when you obtain your license — they set the requirement, and we won’t add conditions they haven’t imposed. If your license requires witness signatures, bring the witnesses; if you need help arranging one, ask us.

What languages do you offer?

English, Spanish, Haitian Creole, and confirmed bilingual combinations of those — English/Spanish or English/Haitian Creole. Availability depends on qualified staffing for your specific date, which we confirm before you sign anything.

A standard single-language ceremony is never surcharged for choosing Spanish or Haitian Creole. Bilingual and custom-translation pricing reflects the extra writing, review, and rehearsal involved.

Can we write our own vows?

Yes, and we encourage it. If you’d like help, we offer writing support and custom vow translation reviewed by a native speaker, so you can say your vows in the language that means the most to you.

How many guests may attend?

As many as you like — we don’t cap guest counts on any experience. Invite two people or fifty.

The practical limit is your location, not your package: parks, venues, and private properties set their own capacity, and some require reservations or permits for larger groups. Tell us roughly how many people you’re expecting and we’ll make sure the location and our coverage fit the day.

What happens if it rains?

We agree on a backup location with you in advance, and your agreement sets a weather decision deadline before the ceremony along with the rescheduling terms. Florida weather is part of planning here, not a surprise — we plan for it with you.

When will we receive our photos and video?

Our target schedule is previews within 72 hours, edited photos within 14 days, and films within 21 days. Your agreement states the delivery schedule that applies to your booking.

Is the marriage-license fee included?

No. Your license fee is paid directly to the county clerk. Venue costs, permits, parking, and third-party vendor fees are also separate unless your agreement expressly includes them.

How do we reserve a date?

Three things, in order: we confirm availability, you sign the agreement, and you pay the retainer. Only then is your date reserved.

Submitting the inquiry form does not reserve your date — it starts the conversation so we can check availability for you.

Do you offer religious and nonreligious ceremonies?

We conduct religious, spiritual, blended, and entirely secular ceremonies. Tell us what your families expect and what feels right to you, and we’ll shape the script around it.

Do vow renewals require a license?

No. A vow renewal is a symbolic celebration of a marriage you already have, so no new marriage license is generally required — and a renewal is not a new legal marriage. Every package works for a renewal; just select “Vow renewal” on the form.
